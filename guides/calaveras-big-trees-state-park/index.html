Where dogs can go

Calaveras Big Trees is here for two groves of giant sequoias, and dogs cannot walk either one. The park allows leashed dogs in the campgrounds, picnic areas, on paved roads and on its dirt fire roads, and nowhere else. The fire roads give you a real walk in the forest, but you will not see a sequoia from them. The good news is that Arnold, 4 miles away, is the start of the Arnold Rim Trail in Stanislaus National Forest, where dogs are welcome on miles of trail.

  • Campgrounds

    North Grove Campground near the entrance and Oak Hollow Campground 4 miles in along the parkway. Dogs must be in a tent or vehicle at night.

  • Dirt fire roads

    The park's own page lists fire roads as the one place beyond the campgrounds where dogs can go. Several miles exist, but they are not signed for visitors. Ask at the visitor center for a map.

  • Paved roads

    The entrance road and the Walter W. Smith Memorial Parkway toward the South Grove, when it is open. Walk the shoulder with care; the parkway has blind curves.

  • Picnic areas

    Most sources, including the park concession and visitor guides, say leashed dogs are fine at the picnic sites. The official page lists only campgrounds and fire roads, so expect rangers to be strict if you wander off the tables.

  • Parking lots

    Including the North Grove and South Grove lots. The groves themselves start at the trail sign and are closed to dogs.

  • Beaver Creek and Stanislaus River picnic areas

    Summer swimming spots along the parkway. Keep the dog on leash and within the developed area.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • North Grove Trail

    The 1.5-mile loop past the Discovery Stump and the Big Stump, and the shorter accessible Three Senses Trail. No dogs, carried or walked.

  • South Grove Trail

    The 5-mile round trip to the Agassiz Tree and the Palace Hotel Tree. No dogs.

  • Every other hiking trail

    Lava Bluffs, River Trail, Bradley Grove and the trails between the campgrounds. Dogs may not go into the woods off a road.

  • Visitor center and buildings

    Dogs wait outside with someone.

  • Groomed ski and snowshoe trails in winter

    The North Grove area becomes a Nordic area when there is snow and trails stay closed to dogs.

  • Group campground, maybe

    Listings disagree on whether pets are allowed at the group camp. Ask when you book.

Good places to hang out

  • North Grove picnic area

    Shaded tables next to the campground and visitor center. The closest you can sit to the big trees with a dog.

  • Beaver Creek picnic area

    Summer swimming holes on the parkway. Mosquitoes in June; bring repellent for yourself.

  • Stanislaus River bridge area

    At the bottom of the parkway, with a picnic area and cool water in July and August. Road closed in winter.

  • Oak Hollow Campground loops

    Quieter than North Grove and set in open forest, good for an evening leash walk.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Dogs are allowed in campgrounds and on fire roads and paved roads. Not on any hiking trail or in the groves.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including at your campsite or in a parked car.
  4. At night, dogs must be inside a tent or vehicle.
  5. Store all dog food in the campsite bear locker, never in the tent.
  6. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can.
  7. Keep dogs out of the visitor center and off groomed winter trails.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

When to go

  • Winter snow

    The park sits at 4,700 feet and gets real snow from December to March. Carry chains. The parkway to the South Grove closes from about mid-November to late April, so Beaver Creek and the river are out of reach.

  • Summer weekends

    The North Grove lot fills by late morning on summer weekends. Afternoons reach the 80s and 90s; walk fire roads early.

  • Prescribed burns

    The park burns sections of forest in fall. A planned burn was set to close some trails and roads from late September 2026. Check the park page before you drive up.

  • Spring and fall

    Dogwoods bloom in May and turn red in October. Cooler, quieter and the best time for the Arnold Rim Trail.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears live in the park and raid campsites. Use the bear locker for dog food, bowls and treats.
  • Rattlesnakes are common on sunny rock and road edges, especially down near the river.
  • Mountain lions are present. Keep dogs leashed and close at dawn and dusk.
  • Ticks and foxtails fill the dry grass from May to September. Check paws and ears after every walk.
