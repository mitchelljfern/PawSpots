Where dogs can go

Death Valley lets leashed dogs go on every road, paved or dirt, and in every campground and picnic area. That is more roadside desert than most parks offer, and dirt roads like Twenty Mule Team Canyon make decent walks. But no trail is open to dogs, not even the paved paths at Zabriskie Point and Badwater, and from late spring to early fall the park itself says not to bring pets at all.

  • Every road, paved or dirt

    The big one. Artists Drive, Twenty Mule Team Canyon, Mustard Canyon, Titus Canyon, Dantes View Road and hundreds of miles of backcountry road. Walk the road on leash and stay close to it.

  • Campgrounds

    Furnace Creek, Sunset, Texas Springs, Stovepipe Wells, Mesquite Spring, Emigrant and Wildrose, with a limit of 4 pets per site.

  • Picnic areas and parking lots

    Including the lots at Zabriskie Point, Badwater, Dantes View and Mesquite Flat Dunes. One person can watch the dog while the other walks out.

  • Harmony Borax Works bike path

    The park's pets page says the paved bike path that runs beside Highway 190 to Harmony Borax Works is the one path open to pets. The interpretive loop around the ruins is not. Confirm at the visitor center; the page is old.

  • Developed areas at Furnace Creek and Stovepipe Wells

    Walkways, the general stores' patios and the campground loops. Not inside buildings.

  • Pet rooms at Stovepipe Wells Village and Panamint Springs Resort

    Both take dogs with a fee. Stovepipe Wells also has a dog run area.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every trail

    Including the paved path to Zabriskie Point, the Badwater and Salt Creek boardwalks, Golden Canyon, Mosaic Canyon, Natural Bridge, Darwin Falls and the sand dunes beyond the parking lot. Carrying a dog does not count.

  • Wilderness and anywhere off the road

    Federal rules keep pets within 50 feet of roads and developed areas here. Do not wander across the salt flats or up a wash.

  • Buildings

    Visitor center, museum, stores and restaurants.

  • The Inn at Death Valley and The Ranch at Death Valley

    Only service animals are allowed in guest rooms at The Oasis properties. The Ranch allows pets in the RV and camping area only.

  • Left in a vehicle in the heat

    Specifically prohibited. A car at Furnace Creek reaches lethal temperatures in minutes, even in spring.

  • Scotty's Castle grounds

    Closed to all visitors for flood repairs; check the park site for reopening.

Good places to hang out

  • Artists Drive and Artists Palette

    A 9-mile paved one-way road through pink and green hills. Pull over at the Palette and walk the roadside with the dog; the short trail up to the overlook is closed to pets.

  • Dantes View parking lot

    A 5,500-foot view over Badwater, often 25 degrees cooler than the valley floor. Dogs can take in the view from the lot and the road edge.

  • Furnace Creek Campground and the Ranch lawns

    Date palms, grass and the only real shade for 50 miles. The reservation-only campground is the best winter base with a dog.

  • Badwater Basin parking

    The lowest point in North America is a few steps from the lot. The boardwalk and salt flat are closed to pets, so take turns.

  • Mesquite Spring Campground

    Quiet northern campground at 1,800 feet with water and flush toilets, cooler than Furnace Creek and close to the Titus Canyon mouth.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Dogs may go on roads, in campgrounds, picnic areas and parking lots only. No trails, boardwalks, dunes or wilderness, even if carried.
  3. Never leave a dog in a vehicle in warm weather. It is prohibited and rangers will act on it.
  4. Never leave a dog unattended at a campsite; coyotes have taken dogs from outside RVs.
  5. No more than 4 pets per campsite.
  6. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can. There are few bins outside developed areas.
  7. The park advises not bringing pets at all from late spring to early fall because of the heat.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Furnace Creek CampgroundThe only campground you can reserve, on Recreation.gov from mid-October to mid-April. Shade trees, water and flush toilets.
  • Sunset and Texas Springs CampgroundsFirst come, first served across the road from Furnace Creek, open fall to spring. Pets are allowed per the park; one booking site wrongly lists Texas Springs as no pets.
  • Stovepipe Wells CampgroundOpen, gravel sites near the dunes and the pet-friendly village hotel. Open fall to spring.
  • Mesquite Spring Campground40 sites at the north end, open year-round, cooler and quieter.
  • Wildrose and EmigrantFree, high-elevation sites in the Panamints. Cold in winter, the only sensible summer camps with a dog.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Stovepipe Wells Village HotelInside the park. Up to 2 dogs of any size with a per-stay fee; reports put it at $20 to $30. Dogs cannot be left alone in rooms.
  • Panamint Springs ResortWest side of the park on Highway 190. Dogs on leash with a small nightly fee.
  • Amargosa Opera House & Hotel, Death Valley Junction30 minutes east of Furnace Creek. Up to 2 pets with a per-pet fee.
  • Longstreet Inn & Casino, Amargosa ValleyDogs on request with a refundable deposit, about 40 minutes from Furnace Creek.
  • Not allowedThe Inn at Death Valley and The Ranch at Death Valley guest rooms (service animals only).

When to go

  • Summer (May to September)

    Highs of 115 to 125 F. The ground can top 180 F and will burn paws in seconds. The park says do not bring pets. Most campgrounds close.

  • Winter (November to March)

    The season for dogs. Days in the 60s and 70s, cold nights, and every campground open. Furnace Creek fills on holiday weekends.

  • Spring (March and April)

    Wildflowers and crowds, and highs already in the 90s by April. Walk at dawn and be back at camp by 10 am.

  • Flash floods and road closures

    Storms have closed Titus Canyon, Artists Drive and Scotty's Castle Road for months at a time. Check current conditions before you plan a drive.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Coyotes patrol Furnace Creek and Stovepipe Wells campgrounds and have grabbed small dogs left outside. Keep dogs inside the tent or RV at night.
  • Rattlesnakes and sidewinders are active in spring and fall, especially at dusk near roads and washes.
  • Scorpions hide under rocks and gear. Shake out dog beds before packing them.
  • Ravens will take food and small items from an unattended campsite.
