Where dogs can go

Humboldt Redwoods follows the standard California State Parks rule: dogs are welcome in campgrounds, picnic areas, parking lots and on park roads, but not on any trail. The good news is that the park's main attraction, the 32-mile Avenue of the Giants, is a road, so you can stop at pullouts and walk the shoulder under the biggest trees in the world. The Eel River day-use areas give you shade, picnic tables and river access too. What you cannot do is walk Founders Grove, Rockefeller Loop or any other grove trail with your dog.

  • Avenue of the Giants (State Route 254)

    The 32-mile scenic road through the park is open to dogs on leash. Park at any pullout and walk the paved shoulder among the trees. Watch for traffic; it is a real road.

  • Campgrounds

    Burlington (year-round, next to the visitor center), Hidden Springs (summer, near Myers Flat) and Albee Creek (May to early September, on Mattole Road). Dogs must stay in your tent or vehicle at night.

  • Day-use and picnic areas

    Williams Grove, the California Federation of Women's Clubs Grove, Dyerville Flats and the other developed day-use areas with picnic tables and restrooms.

  • Eel River gravel bars next to day-use areas

    Williams Grove and the Women's Clubs Grove have river access from the picnic area. Keep the leash on and stay near the developed area; the park's rule is developed areas only.

  • Parking lots and pullouts

    Including the Founders Grove and Rockefeller Loop parking areas. You can see big trees from the lot, but the trail itself starts a few steps away and is off limits.

  • Mattole Road and other park roads

    The paved road west through Bull Creek Flats toward Honeydew is open to leashed dogs on the shoulder.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every trail in the park

    Founders Grove, Rockefeller Loop, Bull Creek Flats, Drury-Chaney, Grieg-French-Bell, River Trail and all the rest. Carrying a small dog does not make a trail legal.

  • Environmental campsites

    No pets at the Hamilton Barn or Baxter environmental camps.

  • Visitor center

    Dogs wait outside. There is shade and a lawn next to the building.

  • Undeveloped forest

    Dogs cannot leave the road, parking lot or picnic area to explore among the trees.

  • Unattended anywhere

    Not at a campsite, not tied to a picnic table and not in a parked car.

Good places to hang out

  • Williams Grove

    The park's biggest day-use area: sunny and shaded picnic tables, grills, restrooms and a path to the river. 3 miles south of the visitor center.

  • California Federation of Women's Clubs Grove

    Picnic grove with the Julia Morgan-designed Hearthstone fireplace and a swimming hole. Storm erosion has closed part of the lot; vehicles over 16 feet cannot enter.

  • Dyerville Flats

    Where the South Fork meets the main Eel River. Restrooms, picnic tables, an overlook and lots of parking.

  • Founders Grove parking area

    You cannot take the dog on the loop, but the lot sits right among old-growth giants, and the visitor center lawn is a few minutes away.

  • Burlington Campground lawn and visitor center area

    Flat, shaded and next to the Avenue. A good place for an evening walk from your campsite.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Dogs stay in developed areas only: campgrounds, picnic areas, parking lots and roads. No trails.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including at your campsite or in a parked car.
  4. At night, dogs must be inside a tent or vehicle.
  5. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can.
  6. Keep dogs out of the visitor center and other buildings.
  7. Store dog food in your vehicle or a bear-resistant locker, never in the tent.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Burlington Campground57 sites next to the visitor center, open year-round. Reserve on ReserveCalifornia. The best base for dog owners.
  • Hidden Springs Campground154 sites near Myers Flat, open roughly Memorial Day to Labor Day. Pets OK in standard sites.
  • Albee Creek Campground40 sites on Mattole Road, 5 miles west of the Avenue, open early May to Labor Day. Quieter, with meadow views.
  • Not allowedHamilton Barn and Baxter environmental camps.

When to go

  • Late summer algae in the Eel River

    Toxic blue-green algae blooms have killed dogs in the Eel River in late summer and early fall. If the water is warm, slow and has green scum or mats, keep your dog out of it entirely.

  • Summer heat inland

    The river flats can hit the 90s in July and August. Under the redwoods it stays cool. Walk early and bring more water than you expect to need.

  • Spring and fall

    The best time. Fewer cars on the Avenue, cool shade, and the river is clean and running. Hidden Springs and Albee Creek may be closed, so book Burlington.

  • Winter rain and flooding

    The Eel River floods in big storms and parts of the Avenue and Mattole Road can close. Burlington stays open year-round, but check road conditions before you drive.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears live throughout the park. Keep dog food in the car or the campsite locker.
  • Mountain lions are present. Keep dogs leashed and close at dawn and dusk.
  • Ticks are common on grass and road edges; Lyme disease occurs in Humboldt County. Check your dog after every walk.
  • Poison oak grows along roadsides and river bars. The oil gets on fur and then on you.
  • Toxic algae in the Eel River in late summer (see Seasons).
