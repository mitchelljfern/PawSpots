Where dogs can go

Joshua Tree lets dogs go wherever a car can go: paved roads, dirt roads, picnic areas and all 8 campgrounds, as long as you stay within 100 feet of the road. Two short paved loops, Oasis of Mara and Keys View, are the only trails that allow dogs. Everything else, from Hidden Valley to Barker Dam to Ryan Mountain, is closed to pets. The upside is that the famous boulder piles sit right beside the road, and camping with a dog here is easy from October to April.

  • Within 100 feet of any road

    This is the main rule. Park Boulevard, Pinto Basin Road and every dirt road count. You can stop at almost any pullout and wander 100 feet toward the rocks with the dog.

  • Oasis of Mara loop

    A flat, paved 0.5-mile loop behind the Oasis Visitor Center in Twentynine Palms, with shade from fan palms. The best short walk in the park with a dog.

  • Keys View

    The paved 0.2-mile loop at the overlook is open to dogs. On a clear day you see the Salton Sea and Mount San Jacinto from 5,185 feet.

  • Dirt backcountry roads

    Geology Tour Road, Queen Valley roads and the Covington Flats and Black Rock roads. Dogs can walk the road itself and up to 100 feet off it, on leash.

  • Picnic areas

    Hidden Valley, Quail Springs, Cap Rock, Live Oak, Split Rock and Cottonwood picnic areas all welcome leashed dogs.

  • All 8 campgrounds

    Jumbo Rocks, Black Rock, Indian Cove, Cottonwood, Hidden Valley, Ryan, Belle and White Tank. Dogs stay on leash and never alone at the site.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every hiking trail except the two paved loops

    Hidden Valley loop, Barker Dam, Arch Rock, Skull Rock, Cholla Cactus Garden, Ryan Mountain, Lost Palms Oasis, Fortynine Palms, Cottonwood Spring and the 3 trails at Black Rock. Carrying a dog does not make it legal.

  • More than 100 feet from a road

    Even on open desert with no trail, pets must stay within 100 feet of the road, parking area or campground.

  • Backcountry and wilderness

    No dogs on backpacking routes or at backcountry campsites.

  • Buildings

    Visitor centers at Oasis, Joshua Tree, Cottonwood and Black Rock.

  • Left alone, anywhere

    Not at the campsite, not tied to a post, and not in the car. Rangers treat a dog in a hot car as an emergency and can fine you.

Good places to hang out

  • Hidden Valley picnic area

    Shaded tables among the boulders, with the Hidden Valley rocks right beside you. The loop trail itself is closed to dogs, but the picnic area is not.

  • Skull Rock pullout

    The famous rock is a few steps from Park Boulevard, well within the 100-foot rule. Go at sunrise before the crowds.

  • Cap Rock picnic area

    Flat paved parking with tables and boulders to sit on. Sunset here is the classic Joshua Tree view.

  • Cholla Cactus Garden pullout

    The loop trail is off limits, but the roadside view of the cholla at golden hour is the same. Keep the dog well back; cholla spines hook into paws.

  • Cottonwood Spring picnic area

    South entrance stop with tables and fan palms. The trails beyond are closed to dogs, but the picnic area and campground are fine.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times, everywhere in the park.
  2. Stay within 100 feet of a road, parking area, picnic area or campground. The only trails open to dogs are the paved Oasis of Mara and Keys View loops.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including tied up at a campsite or left in a parked car.
  4. Pick up waste and carry it to a trash can. Campground bins fill fast; bring extra bags.
  5. Keep dogs away from cactus and cholla. Spines go through paw pads and are painful to remove.
  6. Do not let dogs dig, chase lizards or disturb desert tortoises.
  7. Rangers can issue fines for off-trail dogs and for dogs left in cars. Pet rules are enforced.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Jumbo Rocks Campground124 sites among boulders, the most popular in the park. Reserve on Recreation.gov; no water, so bring it.
  • Black Rock CampgroundNorth edge near Yucca Valley, with drinking water, flush toilets and a short drive to vets and stores. Best base for dog owners.
  • Indian Cove CampgroundOff its own entrance near Twentynine Palms, below big rock walls. Reserve ahead; no water.
  • Cottonwood CampgroundSouth entrance, with water and flush toilets. Warmer and quieter; good in winter.
  • Hidden Valley, Ryan, Belle and White TankFirst-come, first-served, no water. Belle and White Tank close in summer.

When to go

  • Summer heat (May to September)

    Daytime highs top 100 F and pavement burns paws by mid-morning. The park advises against bringing pets in summer. If you must, walk at dawn only.

  • Fall and spring (October to April)

    The sweet spot. Mild days, cold nights, and the campgrounds fill every weekend. Reserve Jumbo Rocks and Black Rock months ahead.

  • Spring wildflowers and crowds

    March and April bring blooms and the biggest crowds of the year. Expect lines at the West Entrance by 9 am.

  • Flash floods and road damage

    Summer storms can close dirt roads and wash out Pinto Basin Road. Check current conditions on the park website before you go.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Coyotes are bold around campgrounds and will take a small dog. Keep the leash short at dusk and never leave food out.
  • Rattlesnakes are active from spring to fall, often on warm roads at night. Walk dogs on the pavement with a light after dark.
  • Cholla and other cactus spines hook into paws and noses. Carry a comb or pliers to remove them.
  • Desert tortoises are protected. Keep dogs well away and never let them dig at burrows.
