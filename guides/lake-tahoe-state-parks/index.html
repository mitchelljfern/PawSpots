Where dogs can go

The four California state parks on Lake Tahoe (Emerald Bay, D.L. Bliss, Ed Z'berg Sugar Pine Point and Kings Beach) keep dogs to campgrounds, paved areas and picnic spots. No state park beach allows dogs, and the famous Rubicon Trail and the walk down to Vikingsholm are closed to them. The one real exception is the General Creek Fire Road at Sugar Pine Point in summer. The reason to come anyway is everything around the parks: the Tahoe Rim Trail, Forest Service beaches like Kiva Beach, and the Coon Street dog beach in Kings Beach all welcome dogs.

  • Campgrounds

    Eagle Point at Emerald Bay, the D.L. Bliss campground and General Creek at Sugar Pine Point. Dogs must be in a tent or vehicle at night.

  • Emerald Bay vista overlook

    The park's own page lists the overlook at the Vikingsholm trail parking lot as open to dogs. It is the classic Emerald Bay view, and the lot fills by mid-morning in summer.

  • Sugar Pine Point paved paths and historic zone

    Leashed dogs can walk the paved roads and paths around the Hellman-Ehrman Mansion grounds in the day-use area. Not the beach below it.

  • General Creek Fire Road (summer)

    The dirt fire road at Sugar Pine Point is open to leashed dogs when it is not being groomed as a ski trail. The one real trail-like walk in these parks.

  • Kings Beach sidewalks and picnic areas

    Leashed dogs are welcome on the paths and picnic lawns at Kings Beach State Recreation Area, just not on the sand.

  • Boats

    Emerald Bay State Park allows dogs on boats, including at the Boat-In Camp, where they must stay in the camp area.

  • Parking lots and picnic areas at D.L. Bliss

    Dogs can be in the campground, picnic areas and on paved roads. The beach and all trails are off limits.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every state park beach

    Emerald Bay beach, Lester Beach and Calawee Cove at D.L. Bliss, the Sugar Pine Point beach and the sand at Kings Beach. Year-round, no exceptions.

  • Rubicon Trail

    The cliffside trail between D.L. Bliss and Emerald Bay is closed to dogs along its whole length.

  • Vikingsholm trail

    The 1-mile walk down to the castle and Emerald Bay beach does not allow dogs. The overlook at the top does.

  • All dirt trails in the parks

    Including Balancing Rock, Lighthouse Trail, Eagle Point trails, Dolder Nature Trail and the General Creek Trail (the single-track, as opposed to the fire road).

  • Groomed ski trails in winter

    Sugar Pine Point grooms Nordic trails when there is snow and dogs are barred from them, including the fire road.

  • Vikingsholm and the Ehrman Mansion

    No dogs inside the historic buildings or on their tours.

Good places to hang out

  • Emerald Bay overlook at the Vikingsholm parking lot

    The postcard view of Fannette Island and the bay, a few steps from the car. Arrive before 9 am in summer or you will not find parking.

  • Kiva Beach (US Forest Service)

    The best dog beach on the south shore: sand, shallow water and a view of Mount Tallac. Leash required on the beach. Gate closes in winter, so walk in from the highway.

  • Coon Street Dog Beach, Kings Beach

    The rocky stretch east of the Kings Beach boat ramp, run by the local utility district. Rocky underfoot in high-water years. Sources disagree on leash rules; read the sign.

  • Zephyr Cove, north end (Nevada)

    Dogs are welcome on the rocky shore north of the marina dock, not on the resort's sandy beach.

  • General Creek Campground

    Open year-round at Sugar Pine Point, so you can walk the campground roads in snow when everything else is closed.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Dogs stay in campgrounds, picnic areas and on paved roads. No beaches and no dirt trails, except the General Creek Fire Road in summer.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including in a parked car; Tahoe bears open cars.
  4. At night, dogs must be inside a tent or vehicle.
  5. Store all dog food and bowls in the campsite bear box. This is enforced.
  6. Pick up waste and put it in a bear-proof trash can.
  7. Dog rules are enforced year-round, including on groomed ski trails in winter.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • General Creek Campground, Sugar Pine PointThe only Tahoe state park campground open year-round, and the one with the dog-legal fire road. Reserve on ReserveCalifornia.
  • D.L. Bliss CampgroundLate May to mid-September. Walk to the trailheads without the dog; the campground loops are pleasant for leashed walks.
  • Eagle Point Campground, Emerald BaySummer only, on the point above the bay. Dogs in the campground only, not on the trails that drop to the water.
  • Emerald Bay Boat-In CampReach it by boat; dogs allowed but must stay in the camp area.
  • Nearby: Forest Service campgroundsFallen Leaf, Meeks Bay and William Kent campgrounds take dogs and sit next to dog-legal trails.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Basecamp Tahoe CityDog rooms for a $45 cleaning fee. Call the hotel directly to book a pet room.
  • Station House Inn, South Lake TahoeDogs only, $50 per pet per night, 2 per room. Heated patio seating welcomes dogs in season.
  • The Landing Resort and Spa, South Lake TahoeOne dog up to 70 lb or two up to 35 lb, $75 per night. On the lake near Lakeview Commons.
  • Fireside Lodge, South Lake TahoeOne pet of any size for $35 per night, $15 for each extra pet. Near Camp Richardson and Kiva Beach.
  • Desolation Hotel, South Lake TahoeTwo pets of any size, $75 per pet per night. Cats OK too.

For the parts of the park your dog cannot visit.

  • Tahoe Best Friends, South Lake TahoeCage-free daycare and overnight boarding. Lets you do Rubicon and Vikingsholm.

When to go

  • Summer parking

    The Emerald Bay overlook lot and D.L. Bliss fill by mid-morning from June to September. Roadside parking on Highway 89 is ticketed and towed. Go early or late.

  • Altitude and cold water

    The lake sits at 6,225 feet and the water is cold all year. Dogs tire faster up here; shorten the first day's walks.

  • Wildfire smoke

    August and September can bring heavy smoke from regional fires. Check air quality before a long walk; dogs breathe it too.

  • Winter

    Highway 89 at Emerald Bay closes during storms. Eagle Point and Bliss campgrounds close for the season. Sugar Pine Point stays open with groomed ski trails that are off limits to dogs.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears are everywhere around Tahoe and break into cars for food. Use the bear box for dog food, bowls and treats.
  • Coyotes patrol the campgrounds and beaches at dusk. Keep small dogs leashed and close.
  • Mountain lions live in the basin. Do not let a dog run ahead on trails.
  • Chipmunks and ground squirrels carry fleas; keep your dog from chasing or digging at them.
