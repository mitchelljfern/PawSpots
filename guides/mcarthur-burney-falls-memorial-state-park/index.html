Where dogs can go

Burney Falls is one of the strictest state parks in California for dogs. The park's own page says dogs are allowed only in the parking lot, on the paved road and in the campground, not on trails or in day-use areas, and it recommends leaving pets at home. That rules out the Falls Loop, the rim trails, the Pacific Crest Trail inside the park and the Lake Britton beach. Several guides say the short Pioneer Cemetery Trail from the campground is the one trail where leashed dogs are tolerated, but the official page does not list it, so ask at the entrance station.

  • Campground

    Leashed dogs are welcome at your campsite and on the campground roads. They must be inside a tent or vehicle at night.

  • Paved park roads

    You can walk the road between the campground, the entrance and the main parking lot.

  • Parking lots

    The main lot next to the visitor center and store. Big trees give some shade, but it is a parking lot.

  • Camping cabin sites, outside only

    Dogs may stay at a cabin site on leash but are not allowed inside the cabins.

  • Pioneer Cemetery Trail (unconfirmed)

    A short trail from the far end of the campground to the pioneer cemetery and on toward Lake Britton. Most guides call it the park's only dog-legal trail; the official page does not mention it. Confirm with a ranger before you go.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Falls Loop Trail

    The 1.3-mile loop down to the base of the falls and back along Burney Creek. No dogs, carried or walked.

  • The falls overlook

    The paved viewpoint at the top of the falls is a day-use area a few steps from the parking lot. Sources disagree on whether a leashed dog can stand there. The park's rule is no day-use areas, so assume no and ask.

  • All other trails

    Burney Creek Trail, Rim Trail, Headwaters Trail and the Pacific Crest Trail where it passes through the park.

  • Lake Britton beach and swim area

    No dogs on the beach. The park's page says this plainly.

  • Picnic areas and day-use areas

    The official page excludes day-use areas, which is stricter than most state parks.

  • Inside the camping cabins, the store and the visitor center

    Dogs wait outside with someone.

Dog-friendly walks

Every walk here is one your dog is allowed on. Distances are round trip unless it says loop.

  • Pioneer Cemetery Trail

    1 mi out and backEasyDirt

    Starts past campsite 75 at the end of the campground and leads to the small pioneer cemetery, with a spur toward Lake Britton. Widely described as the only trail in the park that allows leashed dogs, but not listed on the park's official page. Ask at the entrance station first.

Good places to hang out

  • Campground loops

    Big pines, soft ground and quiet evenings. This is where your dog will spend the trip.

  • Main parking lot shade

    Not glamorous, but it is where the dog waits while the rest of the family walks the Falls Loop. Never leave a dog in the car here; the park bans it.

  • Pioneer cemetery clearing

    If the ranger confirms the trail is open to dogs, the cemetery meadow is a calm spot to sit before heading back.

  • Nearby: Baum Lake, Cassel

    A PG&E reservoir 15 minutes away with flat shoreline paths, fishing and room for a leashed dog to sniff around.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Dogs are allowed only in the parking lot, on the paved road and in the campground. Not on trails, the beach or day-use areas.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including in your vehicle. The park names this rule specifically.
  4. At night, dogs must be inside a tent or vehicle.
  5. Dogs are not allowed inside the camping cabins.
  6. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can.
  7. Store dog food in your vehicle or a bear-resistant locker.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Burney Falls CampgroundOver 100 sites in the pines near the falls. Reserve on ReserveCalifornia well ahead for summer weekends. Dogs OK on leash.
  • Camping cabinsDogs can stay at the site on leash but cannot go inside the cabin. Only useful if you are also bringing a tent or sleeping in the car with the dog.
  • Nearby: Lassen National Forest campgrounds on Hat CreekForest Service campgrounds along Highway 89 south of the park, such as Cave and Bridge, take dogs and sit next to dog-legal creekside trails.

When to go

  • Summer day-use reservations

    The park required day-use reservations from mid-May to late September in 2026 and the lot still fills early. Book before you drive, and remember the dog cannot wait in the car.

  • Summer heat

    Burney sits at 3,000 feet and July afternoons reach the 90s. The campground is shaded, the parking lot and roads are not.

  • Fall

    The best season for a dog trip: cooler, quieter, no day-use reservations and the falls run year-round because they are spring-fed.

  • Winter and spring

    Snow and freezing nights are normal from December to March. Part of the campground stays open. Trails can be icy, which does not matter for the dog since they cannot use them anyway.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears visit the campground. Keep dog food and bowls locked in the car or bear box.
  • Rattlesnakes are common on the sunny volcanic rock around Lake Britton and along the roads in warm months.
  • Mountain lions range through the area. Keep dogs leashed and close at dawn and dusk.
  • Ticks and foxtails are thick in the dry grass from late spring through summer.
  • Mosquitoes near Burney Creek and the lake can be bad in June.
