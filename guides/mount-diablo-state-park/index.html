Where dogs can go

Mount Diablo follows the strict version of the state park rule: dogs are welcome in the campgrounds, the picnic areas and on the paved roads, and on nothing else. Not one trail or fire road is open to them. The payoff is a summit you can drive to with the dog in the car, three campgrounds that take pets and a huge view. For real walking, the regional parks pressed against the west side of the mountain allow dogs on every trail.

  • Paved roads

    North Gate Road, South Gate Road and Summit Road, all the way to the summit parking lot. Traffic is steady and shoulders are thin, so these are for short leg-stretches, not walks.

  • Campgrounds

    Juniper, Live Oak and Junction all take leashed dogs at standard sites. Reports conflict on Junction, so confirm when you book.

  • Picnic areas

    Rock City, Juniper, Live Oak, the Summit lower lot and the Mitchell Canyon staging area all have tables where a leashed dog is fine.

  • Summit parking lots

    The views from the pavement at the top are the same ones the hikers get. Dogs stay on the lot and the paved road, not on the Mary Bowerman Trail or inside the Summit Building.

  • Developed areas

    Parking lots, restroom areas and the staging areas at the gates.

  • Nearby: Diablo Foothills Regional Park

    Not state park land, but it touches the west boundary near Castle Rock. The East Bay Regional Park District allows dogs on its trails, leashed in developed areas and under voice control on the open trails.

  • Nearby: Shell Ridge Open Space

    Walnut Creek city open space with more than 20 dog-friendly trails and views straight up at the mountain. Plan on a leash; sources differ on off-leash rules.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every trail and fire road

    Mitchell Canyon, Back Creek, Eagle Peak, Donner Canyon, the Falls Trail, Mary Bowerman Trail and the Summit Trail are all closed to dogs.

  • Park buildings

    The Summit Visitor Center, the Mitchell Canyon Visitor Center and the restrooms.

  • Rock City sandstone formations

    You can picnic at Rock City with a dog, but the trails and the rock scrambles to Sentinel Rock are off limits.

  • Castle Rock Regional Recreation Area core

    The reservable Castle Rock picnic grounds next door are listed as no-dogs by the regional park district, while the surrounding Diablo Foothills trails allow them. Check the sign at the gate.

  • Any trail, even carried

    Rangers patrol the summit trails and ticket dog owners. There is no small-dog exception.

Good places to hang out

  • Summit lower parking lot

    On a clear winter day you can see the Sierra, the Farallones and Lassen from the pavement. Dogs welcome on the lot and the road; not in the building.

  • Rock City picnic area

    Shaded tables among the sandstone near the South Gate. The best picnic spot with a dog in the park.

  • Juniper Campground overlook

    The campground sits at 3,000 feet with sunset views west over the Bay. Day-use picnic tables too.

  • Mitchell Canyon staging area

    Oak-shaded tables and a $6 gate on the Clayton side. A good lunch stop before a dog-free walk for the rest of the family.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet at all times, or inside an enclosed vehicle, tent or pen.
  2. Dogs stay on paved roads, in campgrounds, picnic areas and parking lots. No trails or fire roads, carried or not.
  3. No dogs inside the Summit Building, visitor centers or restrooms.
  4. At night your dog must be inside your tent, camper or vehicle.
  5. Never leave a dog in a parked car. Summer temperatures on the mountain top 100 degrees and the lots have no shade.
  6. Pick up waste and carry it to a bin.
  7. The park gates close at sunset and the roads are locked; campers get a gate code.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Juniper Campground36 sites at 3,000 feet, a short drive below the summit. Leashed dogs OK. Reserve on ReserveCalifornia up to 6 months ahead. RVs under 20 feet only.
  • Live Oak Campground22 sites under oaks near Rock City on the South Gate side. Leashed dogs OK; the shadiest option.
  • Junction CampgroundSmall tent campground at the ranger station where the two gate roads meet. Park documents say leashed dogs OK; one booking site says no pets, so confirm.

When to go

  • Summer heat

    The mountain regularly hits 95 to 105 degrees from June to September with little shade and no water on the roads. Walk your dog early, keep them in the shade at camp and never in the car.

  • Fire closures

    On Red Flag days the park may close its roads and campgrounds. Check the park page before you drive up.

  • Winter and spring

    The best time for dogs. Green hills, cool air and the clearest summit views of the year, with occasional snow at the top.

  • Tarantula season

    In September and October male tarantulas cross the roads at dusk. Keep dogs from sniffing them; the bite is mild but the hairs irritate.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Rattlesnakes are common on sunny roadsides and around Rock City from spring through fall.
  • Ticks are thick in the grass from winter to early summer. Check your dog after any time off the pavement.
  • Coyotes and mountain lions live on the mountain. Keep dogs leashed and close at dawn and dusk, especially in Juniper Campground.
  • Foxtails cover the dry grass from May onward and lodge in paws, ears and noses.
