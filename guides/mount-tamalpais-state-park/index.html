Where dogs can go

Mount Tam is a patchwork. The state park itself keeps dogs to paved roads, Old Stage Road, the Verna Dunshee loop at East Peak, the campgrounds and the picnic areas. But the Marin Water watershed lands that wrap around the park allow leashed dogs on more than 150 miles of trails and fire roads, and Muir Beach at the bottom of the hill is off-leash. Know which land you are on and the mountain is one of the best dog trips near San Francisco.

  • Paved roads

    Panoramic Highway, Pantoll Road, Ridgecrest Boulevard and East Ridgecrest to the summit. Shoulders are narrow, so this is for short stretches, not long walks.

  • Old Stage Road

    The one dirt road in the park open to dogs. It climbs gently from Pantoll toward the Mountain Theater and West Point Inn, with wide views south.

  • Verna Dunshee Trail

    A paved, mostly level 0.7-mile loop around East Peak with views of the whole Bay. The best short dog walk on the mountain.

  • Pantoll and Bootjack campgrounds

    Both walk-in tent campgrounds take leashed dogs. Sites are a short carry from the parking lot.

  • Picnic areas and parking lots

    Bootjack, Pantoll, Rock Spring and the East Peak picnic tables are all fine with a leashed dog.

  • Marin Water watershed next door

    Not state park land, but it surrounds it. Leashed dogs are allowed on the Marin Water fire roads and trails, including Old Railroad Grade, Eldridge Grade and the lakes district around Lake Lagunitas and Bon Tempe.

  • Muir Beach

    Managed by the national recreation area at the foot of the mountain. Dogs can be off leash under voice control on the sand. Leash up on the approach paths.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every other state park trail

    Steep Ravine, Matt Davis, Dipsea, Cataract, Bootjack and the Coast View Trail are all closed to dogs, carried or not.

  • Steep Ravine cabins and campsites

    The environmental campground and the cliff-top cabins at Rocky Point do not take pets.

  • Muir Woods National Monument

    No dogs anywhere in the monument, including the parking lots and the shuttle.

  • West Point Inn

    You can walk a dog to the inn on Old Stage Road, but not inside the building or onto the deck.

  • The southern half of Stinson Beach

    The National Park Service section has no dogs. The county section at the north end, including Upton Beach, allows them on leash.

  • Upper mountain on Red Flag days

    When Marin County Fire closes the upper mountain for fire danger, the state park closes too. Check before driving up on hot, windy days.

Good places to hang out

  • East Peak picnic area

    Tables and a snack bar at 2,500 feet, right at the start of the Verna Dunshee loop.

  • Bootjack picnic area

    Shaded tables under redwoods and Douglas fir on Panoramic Highway, with a restroom and water.

  • Rock Spring

    A grassy meadow and picnic tables at the Pantoll Road and Ridgecrest junction. Good for a sit with the dog while others take the Cataract Trail.

  • Muir Beach

    Off-leash under voice control on the sand. Small lot fills early on weekends.

  • Upton Beach, Stinson

    The county-run north end of Stinson Beach, where leashed dogs are allowed. Park in the village and walk north.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet at all times, including in camp.
  2. In the state park, dogs may only use paved roads, Old Stage Road, Verna Dunshee Trail, campgrounds, picnic areas and parking lots.
  3. Carrying a dog does not make a closed trail legal.
  4. At night your dog must sleep inside a tent or vehicle, not tied up outside.
  5. Never leave a dog alone at a campsite or in a parked car.
  6. Pick up waste and carry it to a trash can. Bins are at Pantoll, Bootjack and East Peak.
  7. On Marin Water land the leash rule still applies, and the watershed closes to cars on Red Flag days.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Pantoll Campground16 walk-in tent sites a short carry from the lot. Leashed dogs OK. First come, first served; arrive early on weekends.
  • Bootjack Campground15 walk-in tent sites in the trees on Panoramic Highway. Leashed dogs OK. First come, first served.
  • Steep Ravine Environmental CampgroundNo pets in the cabins or the campsites.
  • Alice Eastwood Group CampGroup sites near Muir Woods. Pet policy varies by booking; ask when you reserve.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Mill Valley Inn, Mill ValleyAt the foot of the mountain in downtown Mill Valley. Pet-friendly rooms; confirm the fee when booking.
  • Acqua Hotel, Mill ValleyOn Richardson Bay, 15 minutes from Pantoll. Pets allowed.
  • Mountain Home Inn, Mill ValleyTen-room inn on Panoramic Highway at the start of Old Railroad Grade. Some pet-friendly rooms; ask ahead.
  • Vacation rentals in Stinson BeachMany allow dogs and put you 5 minutes from the county dog beach. Check each listing.

When to go

  • Summer fog and heat

    Mornings are often foggy and cool below 1,500 feet while the summit sits in sun. Carry water either way; there is none on Old Stage Road.

  • Fire season closures

    From roughly June to November, a Red Flag Warning closes the upper mountain and the Marin Water lands. Check the park and Marin Water pages the morning you go.

  • Winter rain

    Old Stage Road and the fire roads turn to mud and small creeks after storms. Pantoll and Bootjack stay open year-round.

  • Spring wildflowers

    April and May are the best months on the open grassland around Rock Spring and along Old Stage Road.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Ticks are everywhere in the grass from winter through early summer. Check your dog after every walk.
  • Rattlesnakes sun themselves on fire roads and rock outcrops on warm days.
  • Coyotes and bobcats live on the mountain and will size up a small dog. Keep the leash short at dusk.
  • Poison oak lines most trails and fire roads. It does not bother dogs, but it rubs off their coat onto you.
