Where dogs can go

Point Reyes is better for dogs than most national parks, but only in the exact spots the park lists. Leashed dogs can walk long stretches of Kehoe Beach, Limantour Beach and the Great Beach from North Beach to South Beach, plus the short Kehoe Beach Trail and a cluster of ridge and bay trails on the park's eastern edge. Everything else, which is most of the headline hiking, is off limits. Snowy plover closures shift the beach boundaries from March to September, so check the current conditions page the morning you go.

  • Kehoe Beach, north of the trail

    Year-round, on leash. The stretch south of the Kehoe Beach Trail is closed to dogs from March 1 to September 30 for nesting snowy plovers.

  • Kehoe Beach Trail

    The 0.6-mile path from Pierce Point Road to the sand. The one trail in the main part of the seashore that dogs can use.

  • Limantour Beach, southeast section

    From the parking lot southeast toward Coast Camp. The northwest spit is closed to protect harbor seals and birds.

  • Great Beach: North Beach to South Beach

    From the North Beach parking lot south past the South Beach lot to the old lifeboat station. North of the North Beach lot is closed March 1 to September 30.

  • Bolinas Ridge, Cross Marin, Tomales Bay and Jewell trails

    These are in the Golden Gate National Recreation Area land along the park's east side, and they take leashed dogs. Also McCurdy and Randall trails and the Giacomini Wetlands paths at Point Reyes Station.

  • Commonweal and Niman Ranch area near Bolinas

    Trails and ranch roads south of the Commonweal entrance road and west of Mesa Road are open to leashed dogs.

  • Roads, parking lots and the Bear Valley picnic area

    Your dog can join you at the Bear Valley Visitor Center picnic tables and lawn, but not inside.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Most trails

    Bear Valley Trail, Tomales Point, Chimney Rock, Abbotts Lagoon, Estero, Sky Trail, Coast Trail and the route to Alamere Falls are all closed to dogs. So are the lighthouse stairs.

  • Drakes Beach

    No dogs, year-round. Elephant seals use it in winter and the cafe area is busy.

  • Beach sections during plover season

    March 1 to September 30: no dogs south of the Kehoe Beach Trail, or north of the North Beach parking lot. The exact lines are posted on the sand.

  • All park campgrounds

    Sky, Coast, Glen and Wildcat camps and the Tomales Bay boat-in beaches are hike-in or boat-in and closed to pets.

  • Tomales Bay State Park beaches

    Hearts Desire and the other state park beaches on the bay do not allow dogs.

  • Visitor centers and the lighthouse

    Bear Valley, Kenneth C. Patrick and the Lighthouse visitor centers are people only. Service dogs excepted.

Good places to hang out

  • Limantour Beach

    The most sheltered of the dog beaches, with the Inverness Ridge behind you and Drakes Bay in front. Walk southeast from the lot.

  • Bear Valley Visitor Center lawn

    Picnic tables, shade and a creek beside the main visitor center. Good staging spot while someone pops inside for a map.

  • Point Reyes Station

    A one-street town where dogs are welcome on most patios and in several shops. Cowgirl Creamery and the bakery both have outdoor seating.

  • South Beach parking lot

    A paved lot right on the Great Beach with restrooms and an easy ramp onto the sand. Less wind than North Beach on many days.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times. The park discourages variable-length leashes.
  2. Stay inside the exact beach sections the park lists. The boundaries are posted on signs at the trail and on the sand.
  3. From March 1 to September 30, keep dogs off the beach south of the Kehoe Beach Trail and north of the North Beach parking lot. Snowy plovers nest there.
  4. No dogs on any trail except the ones the park names, and no dogs in any park campground.
  5. Pick up waste and carry it out. There are bins at the main parking lots only.
  6. Keep well away from elephant seals, harbor seals and tule elk. Rangers close beach sections when seals pup.
  7. Never leave a dog in a car at the lighthouse or Chimney Rock lots. On a sunny day the wind makes it feel safe; it is not.
  8. Service dogs may go anywhere their handler goes. Emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Park campgroundsSky, Coast, Glen, Wildcat and the Tomales Bay boat-in sites are all closed to pets.
  • Samuel P. Taylor State Park, LagunitasThe best option, 20 minutes from Bear Valley under redwoods. Dogs in the campground, picnic area and on the Cross Marin Trail; not on other trails, in the creek or in the cabins.
  • Olema CampgroundPrivate campground at the Olema crossroads, closest to the park. Call to confirm current pet rules before you book.
  • Lawson's Landing, Dillon BeachBeach camping at the mouth of Tomales Bay, 40 minutes north. Check their dog policy when reserving.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Point Reyes Station InnDog-friendly bed and breakfast in the village, walking distance to cafes.
  • Tomales Bay Resort & Marina, InvernessDogs in designated rooms, $50 per pet per night. Right on the bay.
  • Olema HouseDog-friendly hotel at the Olema crossroads, 2 minutes from the Bear Valley entrance.
  • Nick's Cove Cottages, MarshallDog-friendly cottages over Tomales Bay, 25 minutes north of Point Reyes Station.

When to go

  • Plover season (March 1 to September 30)

    Beach boundaries shrink. Kehoe Beach south of the trail and the Great Beach north of the North Beach lot are closed to dogs. Signs mark the lines; fines are real.

  • Summer fog and wind

    Expect 55 degrees and a stiff wind on the outer beaches in July. Limantour and the east-side trails are usually warmer and calmer.

  • Elephant seal season (December to March)

    Seals haul out on Drakes Beach and around Chimney Rock, and the park sometimes closes extra beach sections. Check current conditions before you drive out.

  • Fall (September to November)

    The warmest, clearest weeks of the year, and the plover closures lift on October 1. The best time to bring a dog.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Tule elk roam Tomales Point and the Limantour area. Bulls are aggressive in the fall rut and will charge a dog.
  • Elephant seals and harbor seals rest on beaches from winter through spring. Stay 100 yards back; a dog can panic a whole colony.
  • Ticks carry Lyme disease here and are heavy in the coastal scrub. Check your dog every night.
  • Sneaker waves and rip currents make the Great Beach and Kehoe unsafe for swimming, for people and dogs. Keep the leash on near the water.
  • Poison oak lines most trails. Wipe the dog down before they climb into the car or onto the bed.
