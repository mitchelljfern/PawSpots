Where dogs can go

Redwood is really four parks run together, and the dog rule is the same in all of them: leashed dogs can use campgrounds, picnic areas, roads, parking lots and the drive-up beaches, but no trail at all. The saving grace is Cal-Barrel Road and Walker Road, two quiet gravel roads that take you and your dog right into old-growth redwoods. Add miles of empty sand at Gold Bluffs and Crescent Beach and you have a decent weekend, as long as nobody in the car is set on Fern Canyon or Tall Trees.

  • Cal-Barrel Road

    A 3-mile gravel road off Newton B. Drury Parkway in Prairie Creek Redwoods State Park. The park itself points dog owners here. It is the closest thing to an old-growth redwood hike you can do with a dog.

  • Walker Road

    A gravel road in Jedediah Smith Redwoods State Park, off Highway 199. Quiet, flat, big trees, and it ends near the Smith River.

  • Drive-up beaches

    Crescent Beach near Crescent City, Gold Bluffs Beach near Orick and Freshwater Spit south of Orick. All on leash. Miles of sand with hardly anyone on it.

  • Campgrounds

    Jedediah Smith, Mill Creek, Elk Prairie and Gold Bluffs Beach. Dogs in your site and on the campground roads.

  • Picnic areas and overlooks

    Crescent Beach picnic area, Elk Meadow day use area, Klamath River Overlook and Redwood Creek Overlook. Stay on the pavement at the overlooks.

  • Parking lots

    Including the lots at Stout Grove, Lady Bird Johnson Grove, Tall Trees and Fern Canyon. Your dog can wait with one person while the others walk the trail.

  • Public roads

    Howland Hill Road and Newton B. Drury Parkway pass through huge trees. Dogs can ride along or walk the shoulder on leash, but both roads carry traffic, so keep it short.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every trail

    That includes the headline walks: Fern Canyon, Lady Bird Johnson Grove, Stout Grove, Tall Trees Grove, Boy Scout Tree Trail and Trillium Falls. Carrying a small dog does not make a trail legal.

  • Backcountry campsites

    The hike-in and bike-in camps are closed to pets.

  • Ranger-led programs

    Campfire talks and guided walks are people only.

  • Buildings

    Visitor centers at Crescent City, Hiouchi, Prairie Creek and Kuchel are closed to pets. Service dogs excepted.

  • Elk herds

    Not a written rule, but a real one. Roosevelt elk at Elk Meadow and Elk Prairie will charge a dog. Watch from the car or keep well back.

Good places to hang out

  • Klamath River Overlook

    Paved viewpoint 600 feet above the river mouth. Whales in spring and fall, sea lions all year. Stay on the paved area, not the trail down.

  • Crescent Beach picnic area

    Tables on the grass above the beach. Good lunch stop before or after a Crescent City motel night.

  • Elk Meadow day use area

    Big lot and lawn on Davison Road where elk graze. Picnic with the dog on leash and keep your distance from the herd.

  • Jedediah Smith campground riverbank

    The Smith River runs clear and green past the campground. Leashed dogs can cool their paws from the campground side in summer.

  • Stout Grove parking lot

    Take turns: one person walks the 0.5-mile grove loop while the other sits with the dog among the big trees at the lot.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times, everywhere in the parks.
  2. No dogs on any trail, in either the national park or the three state parks. Roads, campgrounds, picnic areas, parking lots and drive-up beaches only.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including tied up at a campsite or in a parked car.
  4. Pick up waste and carry it to a trash can. Bags are not always provided.
  5. Store dog food in the bear locker at your campsite, not in your tent or car.
  6. Dogs cannot join ranger programs or enter visitor centers.
  7. Keep well away from Roosevelt elk. They are wild, they are big, and they go after dogs.
  8. Service dogs are allowed on trails and in buildings. Emotional support animals follow the pet rules.
  9. Ask at any visitor center about the BARK Ranger program. Your dog can earn a tag for following the rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Jedediah Smith CampgroundOn the Smith River near Hiouchi, open year-round. Walker Road and Howland Hill Road are minutes away. Book on ReserveCalifornia.
  • Elk Prairie CampgroundIn Prairie Creek Redwoods, open year-round. Closest camp to Cal-Barrel Road. Elk wander through, so keep the leash short.
  • Gold Bluffs Beach CampgroundRight on the sand at the end of Davison Road. The best spot in the parks for a dog. Unpaved road, no trailers over 24 feet.
  • Mill Creek CampgroundIn Del Norte Coast Redwoods south of Crescent City. Summer only, usually May to September.
  • Not allowedAll backcountry and bike-in campsites. The 4 developed campgrounds cost about $35 a night and must be booked at least 48 hours ahead.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Crescent City motelsSeveral take dogs, including Motel 6, Anchor Beach Inn and North Coast Inn. Fees and size limits vary, so check the listing.
  • Trinidad and McKinleyville vacation rentalsAbout 40 minutes south of Orick. Many cottages welcome dogs with no fee; some have fenced yards.
  • Best Western Arcata InnAn hour south of the park but a reliable pet-friendly hotel on the way in from the south.
  • KlamathClosest town to the middle of the parks, but we could not confirm a pet-friendly motel there. Call ahead.

When to go

  • Fog and drizzle all year

    Expect 50s and 60s even in August, and fog most mornings on the coast. Great for dogs, bad for drying towels. Rain is heavy from November to April.

  • Summer permits at Fern Canyon and Gold Bluffs

    In recent summers a free day-use permit has been required to drive Davison Road to Gold Bluffs Beach, plus a $12 state park fee. Book ahead on the park website, dog or no dog.

  • Elk rut (September and October)

    Bull elk are aggressive in the fall and will charge a dog. Stay in the car near Elk Meadow and Elk Prairie during the rut.

  • Off-season closures

    Mill Creek Campground and some visitor centers close from October to May. Cal-Barrel Road usually closes to cars in winter but stays open to walkers.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Roosevelt elk live along Highway 101, Davison Road and in the campgrounds. Keep 25 yards back and never let your dog bark at them.
  • Black bears and mountain lions are in all four parks. Use the bear lockers and keep small dogs close at dusk.
  • Ticks are thick in the coastal brush from spring to fall. Check ears and armpits every night.
  • Banana slugs are everywhere after rain. They are harmless, but do not let your dog eat them.
  • Sneaker waves and rip currents hit every beach here. Keep the leash on near the water and never let a dog swim.
