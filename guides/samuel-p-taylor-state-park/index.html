Where dogs can go

Samuel P. Taylor is a redwood canyon in West Marin with one route for dogs: the paved Cross Marin Trail, which runs about 3 miles along Lagunitas Creek through the whole park. Add a campground under the redwoods and a shady picnic grove and you have a relaxed weekend. You do not get the Pioneer Tree loop, Barnabe Peak or any other trail, and your dog can never go in the creek.

  • Cross Marin Trail

    A paved former railroad bed that follows Lagunitas Creek the length of the park, about 3 miles, almost level and shaded by redwoods. Shared with bikes and strollers.

  • Main campground

    Leashed dogs are welcome at the standard family campsites in the creekside redwoods.

  • Day-use picnic grove

    Tables, barbecues and lawn under the redwoods beside the creek, just inside the entrance. Dogs on leash.

  • Paved roads and parking lots

    Sir Francis Drake Boulevard runs through the park, but it is busy and has no shoulder. Stick to the Cross Marin Trail.

  • Nearby: Point Reyes National Seashore beaches

    Limantour, Kehoe and the north end of Point Reyes Beach allow leashed dogs, 20 to 30 minutes away. Rules change seasonally for nesting birds, so check the seashore site.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • All dirt trails

    Pioneer Tree Trail, Barnabe Peak, Ridge Trail, North Creek Trail, Bill's Trail and the Devil's Gulch area are closed to dogs.

  • Lagunitas Creek

    Dogs may not enter the water at any time. The creek has coho salmon and steelhead, and people are kept out from December 1 to June 15 as well.

  • Madrone cabins

    The six park cabins do not take pets.

  • Hike-and-bike campsite

    The shared site for cyclists and walkers is no-dogs.

  • Park buildings

    Restrooms and the entrance station.

Good places to hang out

  • Redwood picnic grove

    Deep shade, picnic tables and a lawn by the creek. The nicest place in the park to sit with a dog on a hot day.

  • Cross Marin Trail bridges

    Several footbridges over the creek where you can watch the water. Stay on the bridge; no paws in the creek.

  • Point Reyes Station

    15 minutes west. Dog-friendly patios and shops, and the start of the road out to Limantour Beach.

  • Upton Beach, Stinson Beach

    About 35 minutes south. The county end of Stinson Beach, where leashed dogs are allowed on the sand.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet at all times.
  2. Dogs may use the Cross Marin Trail, the campground, the picnic area and paved areas only. No other trails.
  3. Dogs may never go in Lagunitas Creek, even on a leash.
  4. At night your dog must sleep inside your tent or vehicle.
  5. Never leave a dog unattended at a campsite or in a car.
  6. Pick up waste and put it in a bin; do not leave bags on the trail.
  7. Quiet hours in the campground are 10 pm to 6 am, and that includes barking.
  8. No pets in the cabins or at the hike-and-bike site; this is enforced at booking.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Samuel P. Taylor main campgroundAbout 60 sites under the redwoods beside Lagunitas Creek. Reserve on ReserveCalifornia up to 6 months ahead. Leashed dogs OK at standard sites.
  • Creekside loop sitesClosest to the water. Some reports say these sites close for flooding in winter; check when booking.
  • Madrone cabinsNo pets.
  • Hike-and-bike siteNo pets.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Olema House at Point Reyes, Olema10 minutes west. Takes 2 dogs in designated rooms for a per-dog fee; dogs cannot be left alone in rooms.
  • Olema Cottages, OlemaListed as pet-friendly. Confirm the policy when you book.
  • Dancing Coyote Beach Cottages, InvernessOn Tomales Bay, 25 minutes away. Pet-friendly cottages.
  • Vacation rentals in Point Reyes Station and InvernessMany allow dogs for a nightly fee. Check each listing.

When to go

  • Winter and spring

    The creek runs high and the redwoods drip. Best time for the Cross Marin Trail, but expect mud on the shoulders and some campsite closures after big storms.

  • Summer

    Cool under the trees even when inland Marin is hot. The campground fills every weekend; book early.

  • Fall

    Quietest season. Dry trail, warm afternoons, cold nights in camp.

  • Creek closure

    Creek access is closed to everyone from December 1 to June 15 for spawning salmon. Dogs are banned from the water all year.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Coho salmon and steelhead spawn in Lagunitas Creek. Keeping dogs out of the water is the law, not a suggestion.
  • Raccoons raid campsites at night. Put dog food in the car or the food locker.
  • Ticks live in the tall grass along the trail edges. Check your dog after each walk.
  • Poison oak grows along the Cross Marin Trail margins.
