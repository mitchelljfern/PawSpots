Where dogs can go

Inside the parks, dogs are limited to campgrounds, picnic areas, parking lots and paved roads. Every trail is closed to them, including the paved paths to the General Sherman Tree, Big Trees and the General Grant Tree. The good news is twofold: the in-park lodges now take dogs, and the Sequoia National Forest that wraps around the parks allows leashed dogs on its trails, including giant sequoia groves of its own.

  • Campgrounds

    Potwisha, Buckeye Flat, Lodgepole and Dorst Creek in Sequoia; Azalea, Sunset, Crystal Springs, Sentinel, Sheep Creek and Moraine in Kings Canyon. Leashed and never left alone.

  • Picnic areas and parking lots

    Hospital Rock, Lodgepole, Wolverton, Big Stump, Grant Grove and Cedar Grove picnic areas. The parking lot at the General Sherman Tree is fine; the path down to the tree is not.

  • Paved roads

    You can walk a dog along the shoulder of the Generals Highway, Kings Canyon Scenic Byway and campground roads. Watch for traffic on blind curves.

  • Pet rooms at the in-park lodges

    Wuksachi Lodge, John Muir Lodge, Grant Grove Cabins and Cedar Grove Lodge now offer dog rooms for a nightly pet fee with advance approval.

  • Sequoia National Forest trails (outside the parks)

    The national forest and Giant Sequoia National Monument surround both parks and allow leashed dogs on most trails, including Hume Lake and sequoia groves like Converse Basin.

  • Pet backpacks and strollers

    Allowed in developed areas only. They do not make a trail legal.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every trail in both parks

    Including the paved General Sherman Tree Trail, Congress Trail, Big Trees Trail, General Grant Tree Trail, Moro Rock stairs, Crescent Meadow, Tokopah Falls and Zumwalt Meadow. Carried dogs count too.

  • Wilderness and backcountry

    No dogs on any backpacking route, including the High Sierra Trail and Rae Lakes Loop.

  • Shuttle buses

    The summer Sequoia shuttle does not carry pets.

  • Buildings

    Visitor centers, the Giant Forest Museum, markets and restaurants.

  • Crystal Cave

    No pets on the cave tour or the trail to it, and never leave a dog in the car at the trailhead.

  • Big Stump Basin and Grant Grove trails

    These are inside Kings Canyon National Park, so the park rule applies even though they sit right by the forest boundary.

Dog-friendly walks

Every walk here is one your dog is allowed on. Distances are round trip unless it says loop.

  • Hume Lake Loop (Sequoia National Forest)

    2.6 to 2.9 mi loopEasyDirt and paved

    Flat lap around a mountain lake 10 miles from Grant Grove, outside the park boundary. Dogs on leash can swim at the shoreline. The one real dog hike within easy reach of the parks.

    Open in Maps

  • Boole Tree Loop, Converse Basin Grove (Sequoia National Forest)

    2.5 mi loopModerateDirt

    Reaches the Boole Tree, one of the biggest sequoias anywhere, on national forest land north of Grant Grove where leashed dogs are allowed. Rough dirt access road off Highway 180.

    Open in Maps

  • Trail of 100 Giants (Giant Sequoia National Monument)

    1.3 mi loopEasyPaved

    Paved loop through a sequoia grove where leashed dogs are welcome. It is a long drive south of Three Rivers via Highway 190 and the Western Divide Highway, and sections were under repair in 2024; check the forest website before you go.

    Open in Maps

Good places to hang out

  • Hospital Rock picnic area

    Low in the foothills near Potwisha, shaded, with the Middle Fork Kaweah close by. Warm in winter when the groves are under snow.

  • Lodgepole Campground and the Marble Fork

    The river runs right through the campground. Dogs on leash can cool their paws at the edge in summer. The Tokopah Falls trail beyond is off limits.

  • Cedar Grove picnic area and Sentinel Campground

    Deep in Kings Canyon beside the South Fork Kings River, with granite walls above. Open late spring to fall only.

  • Grant Grove village lawn and parking

    You can see big sequoias from the road and the picnic area without stepping onto the closed Grant Tree Trail. Pet rooms at the lodge are steps away.

  • Hume Lake (national forest)

    Sandy beach, lake swimming for dogs on leash, and a small store. The best full-day dog outing on a Kings Canyon trip.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times.
  2. Stay in campgrounds, picnic areas, parking lots and on paved roads. No trails, paved or not, and no carrying dogs onto them.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, at a campsite, tied up or in a vehicle.
  4. Store dog food, bowls and treats in the bear locker at your site. Bears break into cars here.
  5. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can or dumpster, not a bear locker.
  6. Dogs cannot ride the summer shuttle bus.
  7. Lodge pet rooms need advance approval and a nightly fee; dogs cannot be left alone in rooms.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Lodgepole CampgroundBiggest campground, by the Marble Fork Kaweah at 6,700 feet. Reserve on Recreation.gov; open spring to fall.
  • Potwisha CampgroundFoothills near the Ash Mountain entrance at 2,100 feet, open year-round. Hot in summer, ideal in winter with a dog.
  • Dorst Creek CampgroundQuieter, in the forest north of Lodgepole. Summer only.
  • Azalea and Sunset Campgrounds, Grant GroveWalking distance to Grant Grove village. Azalea stays open in winter, first come first served off season.
  • Sentinel and Sheep Creek, Cedar GroveDown in Kings Canyon beside the river. Open roughly May to October when the road is open.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Wuksachi Lodge, SequoiaLimited pet rooms, up to 2 dogs, $25 per pet per night. Book early and ask for a pet room.
  • John Muir Lodge and Grant Grove Cabins, Kings CanyonPet-friendly rooms with a $25 nightly fee per pet and prior approval. Cedar Grove Lodge is listed as dog-friendly too.
  • Lazy J Ranch Motel, Three RiversDog-friendly motel 6 miles from the Ash Mountain entrance. Per-pet fee; reports on the amount differ.
  • Sequoia Riverfront Cabins and Gateway Lodge, Three RiversCabins on the Kaweah River with a pet fee per night.
  • Comfort Inn & Suites Sequoia Kings Canyon, Three RiversUp to 2 dogs with a nightly fee and a weight limit. Sources disagree on the limit, so check.

When to go

  • Winter (December to March)

    Chains are often required on the Generals Highway, and the road between the two parks can close. Potwisha and Azalea stay open; snow-covered roads are not open to dogs on foot.

  • Summer crowds and heat

    Three Rivers and the foothills top 100 F in July and August. Base at Lodgepole or Grant Grove where it is 20 degrees cooler, and walk early.

  • Kings Canyon Scenic Byway

    The road into Cedar Grove opens around late April and closes in November. Hume Lake is reachable most of the year.

  • Fire and smoke

    Late summer wildfire smoke can make exercise hard on dogs. Check air quality and park alerts before you drive up.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears are everywhere. Keep dog food and bowls in the bear locker and never in the car or tent.
  • Mountain lions live in the foothills and canyons. Keep dogs close at dawn and dusk and do not let them run ahead.
  • Rattlesnakes are common below 6,000 feet around Potwisha, Hospital Rock and Cedar Grove in warm months.
  • Ticks and foxtails fill the foothill grass from spring into summer. Check paws, ears and armpits after every walk.
