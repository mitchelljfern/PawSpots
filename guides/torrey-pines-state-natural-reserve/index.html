Where dogs can go

Torrey Pines bans dogs completely: not on the trails, not on the beach below the cliffs, not inside a parked car in the lot. It is one of only 14 state park units with natural reserve status and the rule is enforced at the entrance kiosk. This guide is mostly about what to do instead, because the coast on either side is full of dog beaches and leashed canyon trails. One more thing: the reserve itself is closed for construction from September 22, 2026 until about March 2027. Only the beach and its lots are open.

  • Del Mar Dog Beach, North Beach (nearby)

    From 29th Street north to the Solana Beach line, 5 minutes up the coast. Off-leash under voice control from the day after Labor Day to June 15. In summer dogs must be leashed except dawn to 8 am.

  • Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve (nearby)

    A 7-mile canyon with a waterfall, 15 minutes inland. Leashed dogs welcome on an 8-foot leash or shorter.

  • Crest Canyon, Carmel Mountain and Gonzales Canyon (nearby)

    The trails State Parks itself points dog owners to. All within 15 minutes of the reserve; leash rules are set by the City of San Diego and Del Mar, so read the signs.

  • Fiesta Island, Mission Bay (nearby)

    Off-leash across most of the island, 4 am to 10 pm, except the fenced tern nesting areas. About 20 minutes south.

  • Ocean Beach Dog Beach (nearby)

    Off-leash 24 hours a day at the mouth of the San Diego River, 30 minutes south. The oldest legal off-leash beach in the country.

  • Coronado Dog Beach (nearby)

    Off-leash on the sand at the north end of Coronado Beach, 200 Ocean Boulevard. Leash required until you reach the sand.

  • Seagrove and Powerhouse parks, Del Mar (nearby)

    Grass on the bluff above Del Mar's main beach, leashed dogs welcome. A good place to wait while someone else walks the reserve.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every reserve trail

    Guy Fleming, Razor Point, Yucca Point, Beach Trail, Broken Hill and Parry Grove. No dogs, on leash or carried.

  • Torrey Pines State Beach

    The entire beach within the park boundary, including the sand below the cliffs. An unofficial site claims dogs are fine below the tide line; State Parks says no, and rangers patrol.

  • Parking lots and the park road

    State Parks bans dogs even inside a vehicle in the reserve lots. You cannot leave the dog in the car while you hike.

  • Torrey Pines Extension

    The quieter trails across the lagoon in Del Mar Heights are part of the reserve and follow the same no-dog rule.

  • Del Mar Main Beach in summer (nearby)

    Between Powerhouse Park and 29th Street, no dogs at all from June 16 to Labor Day. The rest of the year, leashed except dawn to 8 am.

Good places to hang out

  • Ocean Beach Dog Beach

    Off-leash 24 hours, lively and social. Rinse stations and a big parking lot at the end of Voltaire Street.

  • Coronado Dog Beach

    Wide, calm sand near the naval base with the Hotel del Coronado down the beach. Free street parking at the north end of Ocean Boulevard.

  • Seagrove Park, Del Mar

    A grass bluff above the surf at 15th Street with benches and shade. Leashed dogs welcome.

  • Torrey Pines State Beach from the North Beach lot (no dog)

    If you must see the cliffs together, you can view them from the public sidewalk on Coast Highway 101 at the Del Mar end, outside the park boundary. Do not walk the dog into the lot.

The rules, in plain words

  1. There is no leash rule here because dogs are banned entirely: not on trails, the beach, the road, the lots or inside a parked car.
  2. Service dogs are the only exception. State Parks asks that they wear a vest or tag.
  3. Rangers at the entrance kiosk turn away cars with dogs, and the beach is patrolled.
  4. Never leave a dog in a car nearby while you hike. Lot temperatures top 100 F inside a car most of the year.
  5. At Del Mar, leashes are 6 feet max, dogs must wear a license tag, and leash fines start at $285.
  6. At Del Mar North Beach, off-leash under voice control only from the day after Labor Day to June 15. Summer means leashed, except dawn to 8 am.
  7. At Los Peñasquitos Canyon the leash limit is 8 feet; at Fiesta Island, stay out of the fenced tern nesting areas.
  8. At Coronado and Ocean Beach, keep the leash on until you are standing on the sand.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • No camping at Torrey PinesThe reserve and the beach are day use only.
  • San Elijo State Beach campground, Cardiff20 minutes north. Dogs on leash in the campground and on paved roads under the state park rule; not on the beach. Check current pet rules when you book.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Hotel Indigo San Diego Del MarTwo pets of any size for about $95 per stay, 5 minutes from the reserve. Pets cannot be left alone in rooms.
  • Estancia La Jolla Hotel and SpaTwo dogs for a one-time fee, with pet beds, bowls and outdoor dining where dogs can join you.
  • La Valencia Hotel, La JollaTwo dogs up to 75 lb at about $50 per pet per night. Not in the pool area.
  • Vacation rentals in Del Mar and Solana BeachMany allow dogs and put you walking distance from North Beach. Check each listing.

When to go

  • Construction closure

    From September 22, 2026 until about March 2027 every reserve trail, the park road, the Lodge, the Beach Trail stairs and both upper lots are closed to everyone. Torrey Pines State Beach and its North and South Beach lots stay open. If seeing the pines is the goal, wait for the reopening date on the State Parks page.

  • Summer heat

    The dog alternatives inland, like Los Peñasquitos and Crest Canyon, have little shade and bake by 10 am. Walk early, carry water and never leave a dog in a car.

  • Del Mar's summer switch

    June 16 to Labor Day brings leash rules to North Beach and a full ban on Main Beach. The off-leash months run from the day after Labor Day to June 15.

  • Winter

    The best weather for the canyon trails. Rain can close Los Peñasquitos for a day or two and king tides shrink the dog beaches.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Rattlesnakes are common on the inland canyon trails from spring to fall. Keep dogs on the path and out of the brush.
  • Coyotes live in every canyon on this list and hunt at dawn and dusk. Leash small dogs even where off-leash is allowed.
  • Seals and sea lions haul out on La Jolla's beaches. Keep dogs well away, especially December to May when pups are born.
  • Stingrays rest in the shallows at Del Mar in summer. Shuffle your feet, and keep your dog from bounding through the shorebreak.
