Where dogs can go

Yosemite follows the standard national park rule: dogs stay on pavement and in campgrounds. That still gets you Yosemite Falls, El Capitan Meadow, Glacier Point and a lot of riverbank. What it does not get you is any real hiking, and no lodge or tent cabin in the park takes pets.

  • Paved roads, sidewalks and bike paths

    This is the big one. Yosemite Valley has about 12 miles of paved multi-use path, and dogs are welcome on all of it unless a sign says otherwise.

  • Lower Yosemite Fall loop

    A flat, paved 1-mile loop to the base of the falls. The best short walk in the park with a dog, and loudest in May and June.

  • Bridalveil Fall path

    Short paved walk from the parking area to the viewing spot at the bottom of the fall. Expect spray in spring.

  • Glacier Point

    The paved 0.3-mile path from the parking lot to the famous overlook is open to dogs. Road usually open late May to October.

  • Wawona Meadow Loop

    The one real trail dogs may use: a 3.5-mile dirt loop road around Wawona Meadow, near the Wawona Hotel.

  • Campgrounds

    Every drive-in campground, except walk-in campgrounds like Camp 4 and all group sites.

  • Picnic areas and parking lots

    Including Cathedral Beach, Swinging Bridge, El Capitan and Church Bowl picnic areas.

Where dogs are not allowed

  • Every unpaved trail

    That includes the Mist Trail, Four Mile Trail, Mirror Lake trail, Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall. The paved road to Mirror Lake is fine; the dirt loop around it is not.

  • Mariposa Grove of Giant Sequoias

    No dogs anywhere in the grove, carried or not. Try the paved path at Tuolumne Grove instead, and stay on the pavement there.

  • Shuttle buses

    Dogs cannot ride the free Valley or Mariposa Grove shuttles, so plan to park near what you want to see.

  • Lodging and tent cabins

    The Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, Curry Village, Housekeeping Camp, Wawona Hotel and White Wolf do not take pets.

  • Buildings

    Visitor centers, stores and restaurants. Patios are at the manager's discretion.

  • Wilderness and backcountry

    No dogs on any backpacking route or in the High Sierra Camps.

Good places to hang out

  • Cook's Meadow and Sentinel Bridge

    Classic Half Dome reflection spot at sunset, right off the paved path.

  • Cathedral Beach picnic area

    Sandy river bar on the Merced with El Capitan straight across. Dogs on leash can wade at the edge.

  • El Capitan Meadow

    Lie back and watch climbers on the wall. Stay on the roadside edge, as the meadow interior is closed for restoration.

  • Tunnel View

    The postcard view, from a paved parking lot. No walking required.

  • Tuolumne Meadows (summer)

    Paved road shoulders and the campground at 8,600 feet, cool when the Valley is hot. Tioga Road opens around late May.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Keep your dog on a leash no longer than 6 feet, at all times, everywhere.
  2. Stay on pavement, in campgrounds or in picnic areas. Carrying a dog does not make a trail legal.
  3. Never leave a dog unattended, including tied up at a campsite or in a parked car.
  4. Pick up waste and put it in a trash can, not a bear box.
  5. Store dog food in the bear locker at your campsite, never in your car or tent.
  6. Dogs cannot ride any park shuttle bus.
  7. Rangers can fine you for violations. Fines start at $100 and go up for repeat offences.
  8. Service dogs are allowed everywhere; emotional support animals follow the pet rules.

Camping and places to stay

Camping with your dog

  • Upper Pines, Lower Pines and North PinesIn Yosemite Valley. Reserve on Recreation.gov up to 5 months ahead; they sell out in minutes.
  • Wawona CampgroundSouth end of the park, next to the Wawona Meadow Loop. Best base for dog owners.
  • Hodgdon Meadow CampgroundNear the Big Oak Flat entrance, open year-round. Pets in standard sites only.
  • Bridalveil Creek and Tuolumne MeadowsSummer-only high country campgrounds. Pets OK in standard sites, not group sites.
  • Not allowedCamp 4 (walk-in) and all group campsites.

Pet-friendly lodging

  • Tenaya at Yosemite, Fish Camp2 miles from the south entrance. Pet rooms with a fee, dog beds and a welcome kit.
  • Yosemite View Lodge, El PortalOn the Merced River at the Arch Rock entrance. Pet-friendly rooms.
  • Vacation rentals in Yosemite West and WawonaInside the park boundary; many allow dogs. Check the listing.
  • Rush Creek Lodge and Evergreen Lodge, GrovelandPet-friendly rooms near the Big Oak Flat entrance.

Boarding and dog-sitting

For the parts of the park your dog cannot visit.

  • Tenaya at Yosemite kennel and dog-sittingDay kennel and sitters for guests, advance notice required. Lets you hike the Mist Trail or Mariposa Grove.
  • Yosemite Valley Stable kennelReports conflict on whether the seasonal in-park kennel still runs. Call the park at (209) 372-0200 before relying on it.

When to go

  • Spring (April to June)

    Waterfalls at full roar and the paved paths are at their best. Expect spray at Lower Yosemite Fall and Bridalveil.

  • Summer heat and crowds

    Valley pavement reaches paw-burning temperatures by midday in July and August. Walk early, and bring more water than you think.

  • Reservations

    Peak-season entry reservations have been required in recent summers. Check the park website before you drive up.

  • Winter

    Glacier Point Road and Tioga Road close with snow. The Valley stays open and quiet, and dogs are fine on plowed paths.

Wildlife and hazards

  • Black bears live throughout the park. Keep dog food, bowls and treats in the bear locker.
  • Coyotes hunt in the Valley meadows and will approach small dogs. Keep the leash short at dawn and dusk.
  • Rattlesnakes are common below 7,000 feet on sunny rock and roadsides from spring to fall.
  • Foxtails (barbed grass seeds) fill the meadows in summer and can lodge in paws, ears and noses.
